"how money is created, where credit flows, and what kind of economy those arrangements systematically encourage?"

rbnz.govt.nz "Money creation in New Zealand" etc.

1. the monetary base 'base money' (M0) created by the Reserve Bank (notes and coins) and settlement cash balances held by commercial banks. Settlement cash is used by banks to settle transactions with each other using the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS).
See also https://www.rbnz.govt.nz/hub/publications/bulletin/2022/payments-primer?utm_source=bulletin&utm_medium=document&utm_campaign=money-creation-31-1-23

2. money in daily use, 'broad money' (M2) mostly (98%) in bank deposits. Created by bank lending. Held in deposits: transaction, savings, and term.
See also https://www.rbnz.govt.nz/hub/publications/monetary-policy-statement/rafimp?utm_source=bulletin&utm_medium=document&utm_campaign=money-creation-31-1-23

3. credit creation goes mainly into existing home ownership, 'starving' agriculture and business.

4. As well as changing the OCR, Reserve Bank uses Additional Monetary Policy (AMP) tools including: 'Large Scale Asset Purchase Program' (LSAP) (RBNZ buys government bonds from banks - creating 'base' money - or their clients, creating 'broad' money. When the bond matures it is Treasury's RBNZ account is reduced by the value and the bond is cancelled. Typically, Treasury issues a new bond to cover the reduction in State funds, reducing base money again) and 'Funding for Lending Program' (FLP) (RBNZ lends to banks with interest at the OCR increasing 'base' and reducing bank costs which is intended to flow through to reduced mortgage rates - uncertain).

5. Payment and settlement of broad money transactions, by a complex route, results in reduction of the payers' accounts at their banks and matching increases in the payees' accounts. When this results in funds moving from bank to bank, the BRNZ settlement accounts are adjusted accordingly.

6. Government spending: wages, purchases, interest are paid (with 'Government, ie parliamentary, approval') from the Crown Settlement Account (CSA) held with the RBNZ with no 'overdraft' limit except the 'Government approval', and end up in the payee bank account, thus increasing 'broad' money. Taxation and State fees move the other way. Hopefully the two balance sufficiently to hold inflation. In this context the OCR is a blunt instrument. So, this is also 'money creation' but only a temporary one (but it may be a while) in New Zealand.

7. Banks preferentially create broad money for housing, precisely because a foreclosure sale effectively returns most of the debt. Business loans are perceived as risky in comparison, as indeed they are.

8. The OCR significantly affects house prices by making mortgages less affordable and so reduces the bids for those on sale. The continuing shortage of housing acts to maintain the imbalance. But a high OCR also adversely affects businesses, and hence employment, too.

9. What kind of economy do these arrangements systematically encourage? They encourage what actually happens, of course. Simply put, high OCR decreases house prices and employment. Low OCR increases employment, and house prices. So, either there are more who are out of work so cannot afford the cheaper homes, or there are more in work but unable to afford the dearer homes. The diaspora follows.
